# COMMUNICATIONS

## 0. Introduction and General Principles

### MEANINGS AND SIGNIFICANCE
- **Clearance**: Authorization for an aircraft to proceed under conditions specified by an air traffic control unit.
- **Readback**: The procedure whereby the receiving station repeats a received message or an appropriate part thereof back to the transmitting station so as to obtain confirmation of correct reception.
- **Standby**: Wait and I will call you.
- **Monitor**: Listen out on (frequency).
- **Verify**: Check and confirm with originator.

### ABBREVIATIONS
| Abbreviation | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **ATS** | Air Traffic Services |
| **ATIS** | Automatic Terminal Information Service |
| **CTR** | Control Zone |
| **CTA** | Control Area |
| **ETD/ETA** | Estimated Time of Departure / Arrival |
| **IFR/VFR** | Instrument / Visual Flight Rules |
| **RVR** | Runway Visual Range |
| **UTC** | Coordinated Universal Time |

### SPECTRUM, BANDS, RANGE
- **VHF Band**: 118.000 - 136.975 MHz (Civil Aviation).
- **Channel Spacing**: 25 kHz or 8.33 kHz.
- **Range**: Line-of-sight propagation.
  - Formula: Distance (NM) = 1.23 × (√H_tx + √H_rx) (Height in feet).

### USE OF MORSE CODE
- **Morse Code**: Used for identifying radio navigation aids (VOR, ILS, NDB). Identification is sent via 1000 Hz tone.

### METEOROLOGICAL OBSERVATIONS
- Aircraft shall make meteorological observations when encountering significant weather.




## 1. General operating procedures

### TRANSMITTING GUIDELINES

· Transmitting technique

- Before transmitting, listen for a few seconds to ensure that you don't interrupt any communication
- Not exceeding 100 words per minute
- Maintain the speaking volume at a constant level

· Stuck Microphone awareness: while a microphone button remains stuck, the frequency is unavailable to other operators.

- Ensure that the button is released after finishing the communication!

### TRANSMISSION OF LETTERS, NUMBERS AND TIME

· Transmission of letters

- Phonetic spelling should be used unless there is no risk of this affecting correct reception and intelligibility of the message (alpha, bravo, etc)

· Transmission of numbers

- Transmit each digit separately followed by HUNDRED or THOUSAND (if 00 or 000)
- 10 - One zero
- 75 - Seven five
- 100 - One hundred
- 583 - Five eight three
- 2500 - Two thousand five hundred
- 5000 - Five thousand
- 11000 - One one thousand
- 38512 - Three eight five one two
- For distances, it is permissible to use hundreds and thousands.
- If number is not a distance, then use individual digits
- NOTE: Flight levels are transmitted as individual digits

· Numbers containing a decimal shall be transmitted saying "DECIMAL" at the appropriate time.

- e.g 118'750 -> one one eight DECIMAL seven five zero

· All 6 digits of the frequency must be transmitted, unless if the 5th and 6th elements are 0s, in which case only the first 4 digits are required to be transmitted.

- 118.700 -> one one eight decimal seven
- 118.750 -> one one eight decimal seven five zero

· Transmission of time

- When transmitting time, usually only the minutes are required to be transmitted unless there's risk of confusion
- Use UTC as a reference!
- Time checks shall be given to the nearest half-minute

### TRANSFER OF COMMUNICATION
- **CONTACT**: Change frequency and establish communication (call) the new station.
  - "Contact Control 124.7" -> "124.7, [Callsign]"
- **MONITOR**: Change frequency and listen (do not call until called or necessary).
  - "Monitor Tower 118.1" -> "Monitoring 118.1, [Callsign]"
- An aircraft shall advise the station if it is unable to establish contact on the assigned frequency and request instructions.

## 2. Aeronautical Communications Service

· There are three main categories of aeronautical communication services available:

- Air Traffic Control Service (ATC), provided by Air Traffic Controllers
- Flight information service, provided by Flight Information Service Officers
  - A service aimed at providing information useful for operating aircraft (weather, traffic, state of the runway, alerting emergency services, etc)
  - Terminology used includes "at your discretion". FISOs have no authority over the aircraft.
- A service aimed at providing information useful for operating aircraft (weather, traffic, state of the runway, alerting emergency services, etc)
- Terminology used includes "at your discretion". FISOs have no authority over the aircraft.
- Aerodrome Air / Ground Communication Service, provided by operators who have obtained a certificate of competency

### CATEGORIES OF AERONAUTICAL MESSAGES ORDERED BY PRIORITY

| MESSAGE CATEGORY | RADIOTELEPHONY SIGNAL |
| --- | --- |
| Distress calls | MAYDAY (x3) |
| Urgency messages | PAN PAN (x3) |
| Communications relating to direction finding | e.g VDF or a lost aircraft |
| Flight safety messages | Movement and control messages. 99% of ATC communication belongs to this type.Can also include urgent meteorological messages such as windshear reports |
| Meteorological messages | Meteorological information messages not connected to aircraft safety |
| Flight regularity messages | Operations, servicing, scheduling, etc |

### ESTABLISHMENT OF COMMUNICATIONS

· On the initial contact, the full callsign of the aeronautical ground station and the callsign of the aircraft must be used:

- Barcelona Radar, ECDAF

### CONTINUATION OF COMMUNICATIONS

· When an aircraft or a ground station wants to broadcast information, the message must be preceded by ALL STATIONS

### CLEARANCES AND READBACKS

· Conditional clearances must include the condition (e.g: EZY12AB, behind company traffic, line up and wait runway 27, behind)

### RADIO CHECKS

· Radio check requests should follow this syntax:

- Identification of the ground station being called
- Aircraft callsign
- The words "RADIO CHECK"
- The frequency in use
- Example: Barcelona Delivery, Lufthansa 15Y, radio check, 125'625

· Replies must follow this syntax:

- Identification of the station calling
- Identification of the station replying
- Readability report
- Example: Lufthansa 15Y, Barcelona Delivery, readability 4

· Readability scale:

- 1 (unreadable)
- 2 (readable now and then)
- 3 (readable with difficulty)
- 4 (readable)
- 5 (perfectly readable)

  - Imagine being cleared to/two 5000 feet. It can mean either cleared TO 5000 ft or cleared to 2-5 thousand feet (25.000)

### FLIGHT PLANS

### FLIGHT PLANS

· Pilots may file a flight plan in-flight in a discrete frequency separate from the busy control frequencies (usually the FIS frequency is used)

· Airborne flight plan must contain:

- Aircraft identification and type
- Position and heading
- Level and flight conditions
- Departure aerodrome
- Estimated time at entry point
- Route and point of first intended landing
- True airspeed
- Desired level on airway or advisory route.

· IFR flight plans may be cancelled in flight to change to VFR rules:

- (Airplane) Dusseldorf Tower, G-ABCD cancelling IFR flight plan, intentions are...
- (Tower) G-ABCD IFR flight plan cancelled at ,

### POSITION REPORTS

· Position reports are required at compulsory reporting points, and may be omitted under some circumstances (for example, under radar control)

· Position reports must contain

- Aircraft identification
- Position
- Time
- Flight level
  - Level may be omitted if using SSR mode C transponder
- Level may be omitted if using SSR mode C transponder
- Next position and time over
- Ensuing significant point.
  - Next position and time over, and ensuing significant point may be omitted if prescribed by authority

### DATA LINK MESSAGES (CPDLC)
- **CPDLC**: Controller-Pilot Data Link Communications.
- Used for non-urgent messages (clearances, requests, reports) to reduce frequency congestion.
- Key terms:
  - **LOGON**: Initiation of data link session.
  - **CONNECT**: Active data link connection established.
  - **DISCONNECT**: Termination of data link.

### IFR DEPARTURES

· Traffic departing using a SID must include the following information on initial contact after departure:

- Callsign
- SID designator
- Current altitude
- Cleared altitude or flight level

### HOLDING PROCEDURES

· Normally, holding procedures are published in a chart. However, when pilots are instructed a non-published hold, they can request holding instructions. These instructions must include:

- Fix
- Level
- Inbound track
- Right or left turns
- Time of leg if necessary

## 6. Distress, urgency and comms failure

### DISTRESS AND URGENCY

· Distress vs urgency

- Distress: a condition of being threatened by serious and/or imminent danger and requiring immediate assistance
  - MAYDAY (x3) is used to transmit distress messages
- MAYDAY (x3) is used to transmit distress messages
- Urgency: a condition concerning the safety of an aircraft or other vehicle, or of some person on board or within sight, but which does not require immediate assistance
  - PAN PAN (x3) is used to transmit urgency messages
  - If the flight is a protected medical transportation, pilots must use PAN PAN, PAN PAN, PAN PAN MEDICAL on initial contact.Note that this doesn't apply to commercial flights with a medical emergency on board.
  - Note that this doesn't apply to commercial flights with a medical emergency on board.
- PAN PAN (x3) is used to transmit urgency messages
- If the flight is a protected medical transportation, pilots must use PAN PAN, PAN PAN, PAN PAN MEDICAL on initial contact.
  - Note that this doesn't apply to commercial flights with a medical emergency on board.
- Note that this doesn't apply to commercial flights with a medical emergency on board.

· Distress and urgency messages should be transmitted on the frequency in use initially, unless it is considered that better assistance can be provided by changing to another frequency.

· If a distress message has been intercepted without any response, an aircraft receiving the message must relay it so that the appropriate ground station can receive it.

### ACTIONS BY AIRCRAFT IN DISTRESS

· Report as many as possible of the following:

- Name of station addressed
- Identification of the aircraft
- Nature of the distress/urgency condition
- Intentions of PIC
- Position
- Any other useful information (endurance, POB, fuel on board, etc)

· When distress condition is terminated, PIC must transmit "CANCEL DISTRESS"

### IMPOSITION OF SILENCE

· Silence on the frequency can be imposed by either ATC or the aircraft in distress by saying "ALL STATIONS, STOP TRANSMITTING, MAYDAY"

· Imposition of silence shall be terminated by transmitting "DISTRESS TRAFFIC ENDED"

### ACTIONS IN THE EVENT OF COMMS FAILURE

· Try to establish contact on another appropriate frequency

· Try to establish air to air communication on 121.5

· Transmit blind saying "TRANSMITTING BLIND" and using the words twice technique

· If comms failure is suspected to be caused by a faulty receiver, pilot must say "TRANSMITTING BLIND DUE TO RECEIVER FAILURE" along with intentions twice

· Further procedures will be discussed in Air Law

### ACTION REQUIRED IN CASE OF COMMS FAILURE
- **VFR Flight**:
  - Squawk 7600.
  - Continue in VMC.
  - Land at nearest suitable aerodrome.
  - Report arrival by most expeditious means.
- **IFR Flight** (Instrument Met Conditions):
  - Squawk 7600.
  - Maintain last assigned speed and level for 7 minutes (from Failure or Last Assignment), then adjust to Flight Plan.
  - Proceed to destination aid, hold, and commence approach at EAT (Estimated Approach Time) or ETA.
  - Transmit actions "Blind".

### AERODROME WEATHER TERMS
- **CAVOK**: Ceiling and Visibility OK (Vis > 10km, No clouds < 5000ft/MSA, No CB/TCU, No Sig Weather).
- **NOSIG**: No Significant Change expected (next 2 hours).
- **NSC**: No Significant Clouds.
- **SKC**: Sky Clear.
- **VRB**: Variable wind direction (< 3 knots).
- **NSW**: No Significant Weather.
