# FLIGHT PLANNING

## 1. Introduction

### DEFINITIONS

| Adequate aerodrome | An airport which is equipped with all the necessary services to receive landing air traffic |
| --- | --- |
| Additional fuel | Fuel carried in addition to the basic requirements for specific types of flight operation |
| Airfield | A small location, usually used by general aviation |
| Airport | A large location, usually used for commercial aviation |
| Alternate airfield | A planned diversion airfield for use when the destination airfield becomes unusable while en-route |
| Alternate fuel | Reserve fuel from the destination to the destination alternate airport |
| ATC endurance | The length of time an aircraft can remain airborne before using all on-board usable fuel |
| Block fuel | The amount of fuel that is legally required to complete a route |
| Contingency fuel | Fuel that is carried to cover unforeseen circumstances during the flight |
| En-route alternate aerodromes | An adequate aerodrome along the route, used for planning purposes |
| Extra fuel | Fuel loaded at the Commander's discretion, over and above the legal requirements |
| Final reserve fuel | Reserve fuel usually for holding above the alternate aerodrome |
| Isolated aerodrome | A destination where no suitable alternate exists, sometimes an island destination |
| Leg | A portion of a route between waypoints |
| Optimum altitude | Altitude at which an aircraft flies most economically |
| Safe endurance | The length of time an aircraft can remain airborne without burning any reserve fuel |
| Tankering | A procedure consisting on carrying extra fuel to avoid refueling at the destination if costs are too expensive |
| Taxi fuel | Fuel needed from engine start to take-off, including APU |
| Trip fuel | Fuel needed from take-off to landing at the initial destination |

· Nautical Air Miles (NAM) vs Nautical Ground Miles (NGM)

- Nautical Air Miles refer to the distance flown in relation to the air
- Nautical Ground Miles refer to the distance flown in relation to the ground
  - In still air, NAM and NGM are equal
  - In a headwind, TAS > GS so NAM > NGM
  - In a tailwind, GS > TAS so NGM > TAS
- In still air, NAM and NGM are equal
- In a headwind, TAS > GS so NAM > NGM
- In a tailwind, GS > TAS so NGM > TAS
- Converting NGM to NAM and viceversa
  - NAM = NGM x TAS/GS
  - NGM = NAM x GS/TAS
- NAM = NGM x TAS/GS
- NGM = NAM x GS/TAS

## 2. Fuel Planning & Monitoring

· Operators must establish a fuel policy for the purpose of flight planning. This planning must be based on both:

- Operations Manual or AFM
- Operating conditions of the flight

· Fuel types

| Taxi | Fuel for all operations before take-off (APU, engine start and taxi to the runway) |
| --- | --- |
| Trip | Fuel used between take-off and landing at the destination |
| Contingency fuel | Fuel for unexpected changes to the trip (ATC constraints for example)It is the higher of:1)5% of trip fuel if no en-route alternates are available3% of trip fuel if en-route alternates are available (known as 3% ERA)2)5 minutes at holding speed at 1500 ft above the destination aerodrome |
| Alternate reserve | Fuel needed from missed approach point to the destination alternate |
| Final reserve | Fuel to hold at 1500 ft above the destination alternate:30 minutes for turbine-powered aircraft45 minutes for piston-engine aircraft |
| Additional reserve | Fuel for unusual operations requiring it. |
| Minimum additional reserve | 15 minutes of fuel only required when the fuel on-board isn't enough in case of an emergency or the flight is operated without a destination alternate aerodrome |
| Extra | Fuel loaded at the PIC discretion |

### REDUCED CONTINGENCY FUEL PROCEDURE (RCF)

· Imagine if long-haul flights had to use 5% of their trip fuel as contingency fuel. This would mean that for a B747 flying from London to New York has to carry more than 3000 kg just for contingency reasons. It's too much increased weight when you already have 70000 kg on board.

· Operators are allowed to use a Reduced Contingency Fuel procedure, allowing an increase of traffic load

· Whenever we use a RCF procedure, we need a decision point. This is a point where the crew needs to take the decision of either continuing to the destination of deviating to an alternate.

- Contingency Fuel is 5% of the estimated burn from the decision point to the destination.

### PRE-DETERMINED POINT PROCEDURE (PDP)

· Predetermined Point Procedure (PDP) - used to set a point in the flight where the pilot must decide whether to continue or divert to an alternate (usually because the aircraft has insufficient fuel aboard to reach the alternate from any point closer to the destination). This normally involves obtaining the latest forecasts and estimates of the status of the destination in order to decide whether a safe landing can be made.

### ISOLATED AERODROME PROCEDURE

· Aircraft flying to an isolated aerodrome are required to carry additional fuel:

- Piston aircraft: the lesser of:
  - Fuel to fly for 45 minutes plus 15% of planned flight time at cruise level
  - 2 hours (whichever is less)
- Fuel to fly for 45 minutes plus 15% of planned flight time at cruise level
- 2 hours (whichever is less)
- Jet aircraft: fuel to fly for 2 hours at normal cruise consumption above aerodrome

### FUEL TANKERING

· Fuel tankering: consists on refueling enough fuel for 2 flights. Convenient when fuel is significantly more expensive at the destination than at the departure.

· Fuel price ratio = Fuel price at departure / Fuel price at destination

## 4. Significant points

### POINT OF EQUAL TIME

· Point where time to continue to destination is equal to the time to get back to the departure airport.

| · Distance to PET (nm)= DH / (O + H)D: Total distanceH: GS HomeboundO: GS Outbound |  |
| --- | --- |

· Example:

- Sector of 700 nm
- Wind component outbound = -20 kts
- TAS = 480 kts
- OEI TAS = 240 kts

· Solution

- We first calculate groundspeed out (460) and groundspeed in (500)
- PET = 700 x 500 / (460 + 500) = 364 nm
- This means that the distance from departue to PET is 364 nm

### POINT OF SAFE RETURN (PSR)

· Point where a return to the departure airport would still leave the fuel reserves intact on arrival at the departure airport.

- It depends on fuel endurance
- Typically used in isolated aerodromes or remote regions, and allows to decide whether to continue or turn back

| · Distance to PSR (nm) = EOH / (O + H)E: Safe endurance in DECIMAL HOURSO: GS OutboundH: GS Homebound |  |
| --- | --- |

· Example:

- Safe endurance 6:30 hours
- TAS 400 kts
- WC out +40 kts
- WC homebound -35 kts

· Solution

- Distance to PSR = 6.5 * 440 * 405 / (440 + 405)
- Distance to PSR = 1370 nm
- If we have to calculate the time:
  - We need to fly 1370 nm at a GS of 440 kts, therefore, it takes us 3.11 hours (which is 3 hours and 7 minutes)to get to the PSR
- We need to fly 1370 nm at a GS of 440 kts, therefore, it takes us 3.11 hours (which is 3 hours and 7 minutes)to get to the PSR

### POINT OF SAFE RETURN WITH VARYING FUEL FLOWS

· If we have to calculate the PSR with different fuel flows (which may happen as a result of an engine failure, for example), we have to follow these steps:

1) Calculate the fuel available for safe endurance if not specified in the question

- This usually involves deducting 30 minutes of final reserve to the total endurance if we are flying a jet

2)Calculate specific fuel consumption (kg burnt / miles flown) out and home, and add them

3) Divide the fuel available by the combined SFC

· Example

- Block fuel: 20000 kg
- Reserve fuel: 3000 kg
- Outbound: GS 500 kt, fuel flow 2400 kg/hour
- Inbound: GS 300 kt, fuel flow 1500 kg/hour

· Solution

- First, we calculate that the fuel available for safe endurance is 17000 kg
- Then, we calculate SFC out and home (note that the per hour component is mathematically cancelled):
  - Outbound: 2400 kg / 500 kt = 4'8 kg per nm
  - Inbound: 1500 kg / 300 kt = 5 kg per nm
- Outbound: 2400 kg / 500 kt = 4'8 kg per nm
- Inbound: 1500 kg / 300 kt = 5 kg per nm
- 17.000 kg available / (4'8 kg per nm + 5 kg per nm) = 1734 nm
- Again, we have ground distance and groundspeed, so we can calculate time
